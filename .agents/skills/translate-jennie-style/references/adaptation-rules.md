# Adaptation rules

The goal is aesthetic transfer with user agency. Preserve the styling relationship and retain the original intensity when desired. Change garment cut, exposure, heel, or price only when the user's fit evidence, chosen scene, movement needs, weather, dress code, budget, or preference calls for it. “Ordinary person” versus “celebrity” is not a valid reason to dilute a look.

## Translation order

Apply decisions in this order:

1. **User intention and exact scene:** ask what the user wants to express and distinguish beach, nightlife, festival, date, travel, commute, and other contexts instead of collapsing them into “daily life.”
2. **Climate and wear phase:** distinguish outdoor transit, outdoor destination, and indoor destination; temperature changes the layering plan before it changes the requested styling identity.
3. **Desired intensity and exposure:** never exceed the user's limit, but do not automatically lower a high-intensity preference.
4. **Fit evidence:** use reported garment behavior before optional measurements or photographs; no body category disqualifies a rise, hem, or styling device.
5. **Jennie styling device:** choose the devices that serve the user's intention; several may coexist when the user wants a maximal look and the hierarchy stays readable.
6. **Focal point:** decide what leads and what supports, even in a bold outfit.
7. **Practical execution:** describe cut, material, security, movement, and layering options without making a required brand or modesty assumption.

## Core devices and scene-aware translations

| Jennie styling device | Preserve | Same-intensity interpretation | Optional modulation when requested |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fitted/short base + oversized outer layer | contrast in volume | bralette, camisole, fitted crop or bodysuit under a boxy jacket | use a full-length fitted inner only if more coverage is wanted |
| Minimal top + relaxed low-rise bottom | actual low junction, exposed or visible waistband, relaxed attitude | recommend a real low-rise trouser first when the user likes low rise; test the cut rather than the user's eligibility to wear it | offer mid-rise with a lowered visual junction only when the user requests it or the actual rise causes a reported fit problem |
| Feminine code + utilitarian piece | deliberate tension | lace, sheer fabric, pearls, feather, or underwear with denim, bomber, leather, cargo details, or boots | reduce the number of signals only if the hierarchy becomes unreadable, not because the look is bold |
| Neutral base + one narrative color | color hierarchy | black/white/denim/brown base plus one red, blue, green, or pink focus | add more colors when the intended theme is maximal, but still rank them |
| Monochrome + texture/hardware | depth without color noise | combine lace, sheer, knit, denim, leather-like texture, fringe, suede-like texture, or metal | simplify texture only for the requested scene or comfort |
| Structural accessory | intentional punctuation or architecture | use one or several belts, chains, boots, hats, sunglasses, or jewelry pieces with a clear lead/support relationship | reduce only when accessories compete equally or impede movement |
| Stage micro/brief proportion | compact focal base and exposure placement | underwear-as-outerwear, micro shorts, mini, sheer skirt, or open low-rise layering can transfer directly to beach, nightlife, festival, performance, or any chosen compatible setting | add lining, tights, secure closures, or longer layers only when the user wants them |

## Scenario is not a modesty ladder

- **Beach or island holiday:** bikini or underwear-like tops, lace, sheer skirts, open shirts, low rise, and high slits can be direct translations. Check wind, water, sunscreen, and secure fastening—not whether the wearer is a celebrity.
- **Nightlife:** underwear-as-outerwear, low-rise trousers, micro hems, sheer panels, tall boots, and stacked hardware can remain high-intensity. Check dancing, sitting, venue rules, and coat/bag handling.
- **Festival or concert:** stage formulas may transfer at nearly full intensity. Adjust footwear and closures for standing, crowds, weather, and movement.
- **Date, travel, or casual wear:** intensity is a personal choice, not a fixed low setting. Offer both quiet and bold routes when the user has not decided.
- **Commute or formal settings:** use the real dress code as the constraint; do not invent one.

Movement and seated checks are garment engineering, not permission tests. They should improve security and comfort without implying that bold clothing is inappropriate.

## Climate is a layering problem, not a modesty ladder

Record temperature and wear environment separately.

- **Temperature:** `hot_humid`, `mild`, `cold`, or `unsure`.
- **Wear environment:** `indoor`, `indoor_brief_outdoor`, `mixed`, `outdoor`, or `unsure`.

Combine them as follows:

- Hot + outdoor/mixed: high exposure can transfer directly. Favor breathable, quick-drying, light or open-weave materials and check sun, sweat, wind and fastening.
- Mild + any environment: preserve the requested look; add only a light removable outer layer when useful.
- Cold + indoor: style the indoor destination at full requested intensity and give a coat, transport and coat-check plan.
- Cold + indoor/brief outdoor: keep the destination look at full intensity; add long outerwear, warm footwear and an easy removal plan for transit.
- Cold + mixed: build two readable states from the same formula—outerwear-on and outerwear-off—without hiding the core junction or material tension.
- Cold + outdoor: sustained exposed skin may be thermally impractical. Preserve the real low rise, silhouette junction, lingerie code or hard-soft tension, but use warm long outerwear, lined boots, tights, thin thermal layers or sheer-over-warm construction.
- Any unknown field: give a warm/cold or indoor/outdoor switch only when useful, or ask for the missing field if it would materially change the plan.

Do not say “autumn/winter means high exposure is unsuitable” as a universal rule. A cold outdoor date and a heated indoor date are different wear phases. Reduce sustained exposure only for temperature, wind, rain, movement, or the user's stated comfort—not because the look is bold.

## Fit-response mappings

These are hypotheses for garment selection, not body classifications.

Height alone does not determine a “best” hem, rise, or silhouette. Use the user's recurring sleeve/trouser-length experience and real try-on checks; never convert height into a petite/tall body label automatically.

### Tops and jackets

- `shoulder_ok_chest_tight`: keep a defined shoulder; choose darts, stretch, an open front, or extra chest ease. Avoid solving chest fit by greatly enlarging the shoulder.
- `chest_ok_shoulder_waist_loose`: seek adjustable waist, cropped/boxy intentional cuts, or tailoring; do not confuse excess fabric with desired oversize.
- `shoulder_tight`: prioritize shoulder and upper-back mobility. Use dropped shoulders only when the whole garment is intentionally relaxed.
- `sleeves_long`: retain an oversized effect through body volume while shortening or turning back sleeves.
- `sleeves_short`: choose bracelet sleeves intentionally or size/cut for adequate sleeve length.
- `tops_bulky`: reduce fabric thickness and layered bulk; keep contrast through outline, color, or hardware.

### Trousers

- `waist_ok_hip_thigh_tight`: choose more hip/thigh ease, pleats placed away from the widest point, or relaxed straight/wide legs; adjust the waist separately.
- `hips_ok_waist_loose`: use waist alteration, internal tabs, or a belt; do not size down if it compromises seat/hip ease.
- `rise_issue`: test seated comfort and front/back rise, then try a different low-rise cut, waistband shape, or size before abandoning low rise. A visually lowered belt or longer top line is an optional alternative only if the user wants it.
- `pants_long`: hem or choose petite/inseam options; preserve pooling only when deliberate and safe with the shoe.
- `pants_short`: use a deliberate ankle break or seek longer inseams; avoid an accidental near-full length.

### Waist placement

- Garment waist above natural waist: avoid stacking another strong high belt unless elongating the lower body is a chosen goal.
- At natural waist: belts and cropped outerwear can define the junction cleanly.
- Below natural waist: raise the visual junction with an open jacket, short knit, tonal belt, or front tuck if desired; do not require actual high-waisted garments.

## Goal mappings

- **Longer, cleaner lower line:** tonal trouser/shoe, controlled hem break, higher visual junction, shorter outer layer or open long vertical.
- **Less upper-body bulk:** lighter fabrics, cleaner neckline, open front, one layer of volume at a time.
- **Defined waist:** end or fasten a layer near the natural waist; use shaped seams, a tonal belt, or contrast between compact and relaxed pieces.
- **More ease at hips/thighs:** preserve waist fit separately; use fabric that skims, not grips, with enough seat and thigh ease.
- **More shoulder presence:** deliberate shoulder seam, short boxy jacket, or controlled epaulet; balance with a simpler lower half.
- **More curves:** create junctions and texture contrast rather than forcing tightness everywhere.
- **Cleaner lines:** reduce competing hems, colors, and accessories; repeat one color and choose one focal device.

## Exposure preference spectrum

This is not a hierarchy of safety, taste, or suitability. It records what the user wants for a specific look and scene.

- **Low:** no required skin exposure. Use neckline shape, compact silhouette, sheer-over-opaque, or a belt to recreate the same focal placement.
- **Medium:** often emphasizes one zone—collarbone/arms, waist, back, or leg—but may combine zones when desired.
- **High:** short, low-rise, underwear-as-outerwear, backless, sheer, high-slit, or mini elements may be first-choice recommendations. Include movement and fastening checks without treating them as reasons to lower intensity.

## Output language

Use:

- “Based on the fit issue you reported…”
- “A medium-confidence hypothesis is…”
- “Keep the actual low rise and exposure you want; adjust only the cut and fastening needed for this activity.”

Avoid:

- body-type diagnoses;
- promises to look thinner/taller;
- moralizing modesty or exposure, or treating celebrity status as permission to dress boldly;
- assuming the user's budget, gender expression, or desire to appear conventionally feminine.
