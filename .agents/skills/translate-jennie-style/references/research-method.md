# Research method

## Scope

The bundled snapshot covers 2024-09-01 through 2026-08-31 and samples airport/off-duty, stage/performance, and formal/brand-event dressing. It is a curated teaching corpus, not a complete wardrobe archive.

For a future “last 1–2 years” request, recalculate the date window from the current date and refresh sources that fall outside it.

## Evidence ladder

1. **Tier A — first party:** official performance/fashion video, direct interview, designer/brand documentation, or Jennie's own public post when accessible.
2. **Tier B — strong editorial:** reputable fashion publication with dated photographs and explicit garment description.
3. **Tier C — supporting editorial:** secondary identification or commentary. Use to corroborate, not to anchor a major conclusion alone.

## Record observations

For each look, capture:

- date and scene;
- visible garments and accessories;
- silhouette, waist placement, hem length, volume distribution;
- palette and material contrast;
- footwear, bag, jewelry, headwear, hair/makeup when relevant;
- source IDs and evidence tier;
- one ordinary-person translation;
- confidence.

Keep three layers separate:

- **Observation:** directly visible or explicitly described.
- **Identification:** a publication or brand names the item/designer.
- **Inference:** interpretation of the styling function, such as “the short inner layer balances the oversized jacket.”

## Sampling guardrails

- Include at least five looks per main scene before making cross-scene claims.
- Avoid deriving a rule from repeated coverage of the same outfit.
- Distinguish costume needs from everyday dressing.
- Record counterexamples; Jennie does not use one fixed silhouette.
- State whether a conclusion is frequent, occasional, or speculative.

## Source hygiene

- Save page title, publisher, publication date, event/look date when known, and canonical URL.
- Do not copy full articles or reproduce editorial photographs in the repository.
- Paraphrase. Use short attributed quotations only when necessary and legally permitted.
- Mark inaccessible, deleted, or paywalled evidence rather than silently substituting assumptions.
- Cite live sources in user-facing research reports when browsing is available.

## Analysis sequence

1. Filter to the requested date and scenes.
2. Describe each look without interpretation.
3. Tag recurring styling devices.
4. Compare frequencies and counterexamples by scene.
5. Form a rule only when multiple independent looks support it.
6. Assign confidence and state the evidence boundary.
7. Translate the rule using `adaptation-rules.md`.
